Wash less, wear longer.

Simple ways to keep wool and cotton soft for years, not seasons.

Most clothes are washed out long before they are worn out. Heat, detergent and the drum do more damage in a year than wearing does in five. So the first rule of care is also the cheapest one: wash less.

Air it first

Wool cleans itself better than most people expect. The fibre carries a natural wax that lets go of smells once it has air around it. Hang a knit over a chair back for the night, by an open window if you can, and it is ready again by morning.

Cotton is less forgiving, but a jacket or a pair of trousers rarely needs a wash after one day. Hang them up and let the creases drop. Deal with a mark where it is: a damp cloth and a little soap, dabbed and never rubbed.

When you do wash

Cold water does most of the work. Thirty degrees is enough for cotton, and wool wants it cooler still. Turn everything inside out, close the zips and use half the detergent the bottle suggests. More soap does not mean cleaner clothes. It means stiffer ones.

Leave fabric softener on the shelf. It coats the fibres, flattens wool and strips the wax from a Harbor Jacket.

A hanger will stretch the shoulders of a knit by a size before the season is out.

Dry flat, mend early

Knits dry flat on a towel, in the shade, eased back into shape while they are still damp. Cotton can hang. Nothing we make wants a hot dryer.

Look your clothes over while they are clean. A loose thread or an open seam takes five minutes to fix this week and a new garment to fix next year. If it is one of ours, send it back to the studio: repairs are free for two years.
