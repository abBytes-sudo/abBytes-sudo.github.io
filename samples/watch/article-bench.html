Thirty-eight days at one bench

Every Ghatika is assembled, regulated and cased by one watchmaker. Here is what those days look like.

27 August 2026 · 5 minute read

Our watchmakers do not work on a line. Each watch is assigned to one person from the moment its movement parts are inspected to the moment it is cased and signed. That person’s initials are engraved inside the caseback, under the sapphire, where only a service watchmaker will ever see them.

Days one to nine are finishing. Every bridge is bevelled by hand at forty-five degrees with a file, then polished with diamond paste on a stick of gentian wood until the edge is a mirror. Côtes de Genève stripes go on next, then perlage on the main plate, a few thousand overlapping circles laid down one by one.

“The watch is regulated in five positions over six days. It has to earn its dial.”

Assembly takes about a week. Then come six days of regulation in five positions and at two temperatures, until the watch keeps time within four seconds a day. Only after that does it receive its dial and hands, and only after a final seventy-two-hour run on a winding machine is it cased.

Written at the bench in Udaipur
