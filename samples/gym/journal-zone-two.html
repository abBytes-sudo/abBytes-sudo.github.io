The case for boring zone 2

Most of our members’ easy sessions are not easy. Slowing down is the fastest way to a bigger engine, and the hardest thing we ask anyone to do.

Mateo Rios

Engine & conditioning · 4 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

When we give new members a heart-rate strap for Engine Room, the first week is always the same. Their “easy” intervals sit at 165 beats per minute. They feel virtuous, they sweat a lot, and in eight weeks they have improved less than the person next to them who looked like they were barely trying.

What zone 2 actually is

Zone 2 is the effort where you can still talk in full sentences but would rather not sing. For most of our members it sits between 130 and 150 bpm. It trains the mitochondria and the heart’s stroke volume, the plumbing that every hard effort draws on later. It is boring by design.

“Zone two is boring. That’s the point.”

Easy days that are actually easy are what make hard days possible. If you blur them, you end up with a week of medium-hard sessions: too hard to build the base, too easy to build speed.

How we program it at Forge

  • Two Engine Room sessions a week, one of them mostly zone 2.
  • Tempo Run Club pace groups set by your strap, not your ego.
  • A monthly 20-minute test on the rower to see the engine grow.

Give it eight weeks. Your pace at the same heart rate will drop, and your HYROX runs will start to feel like recovery between stations, which is exactly what they should be.
